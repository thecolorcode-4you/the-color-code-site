FAQ & Policies

The Color Code FAQ: what we do, what it costs and how to start

Short, plain answers for customers, chapters and anyone comparing color analysis options. Everything here is true as of November 2026.

The ten questions people ask most

What is The Color Code?

The Color Code is a free personal color analysis you take on your phone. You answer seven quick questions, add a selfie if you like, and get your color season (one of twelve), a palette of colors to wear and to skip, and matching lip, blush and eye shades.

Who is The Color Code for?

We built it for Tulane students, especially sorority members choosing colors for formals, bid day and photos, and for chapters that want an easy sisterhood event. Anyone can take it online, wherever they live.

How much does it cost?

It is free for everyone during our Tulane pilot, and there is nothing to pay at any step. A chapter's first color night is also free, in exchange for posting your results and tagging us. We have not set prices for after the pilot; see Pricing.

Where are you located?

We are five Tulane University students in New Orleans, Louisiana. The analysis runs on your phone, so you don't need to come anywhere, and chapter color nights happen at your chapter's own event.

How do I start?

Open Start Your Analysis, create a free account with your email and a password, and answer the seven questions. Your result appears in about a minute and is saved to your account.

How long does it take?

About a minute: seven quick questions, then your result. With a selfie, Bella takes about 15 seconds to read your photo. Your palette stays on your phone and in your account.

What happens to my photo?

Your selfie is optional. If you take one, it is used once, only to read your skin, eye and hair color, and deleted as soon as your result is back; we never save it, and no one on our team ever sees it. Your seven answers alone still give you a season.

Who does my analysis, and how is my season chosen?

Bella is our AI agent, not a person: she only describes what she sees in your selfie, such as undertone, depth, contrast and lighting. Fixed season rules our team wrote then choose your season from that and your seven answers, the same way for everyone. Lighting matters most, so if your result looks off, retake it by a window for free.

How does it compare with an in-person color analysis?

In an in-person draping session, a trained analyst holds fabric swatches next to your face, usually by appointment and for a fee. We don't drape: The Color Code is free, takes about a minute on your phone and needs no appointment. Draping gives you a person's eye; we give you your season fast, free and private.

How is it different from free online color quizzes?

Many online quizzes give you a season from one or two questions, such as vein color. We ask seven, weigh each one with fixed rules, can add a reading of your selfie, and give you a full palette with makeup shades and the reasons for your season. See how we weigh each sign.

More questions and our policies

Can my chapter do a color night?

Yes. Every member takes the quiz on her own phone, and your chapter sees who shares a season. During the Tulane pilot, your chapter's first color night is free in exchange for posting your results and tagging us. Message us on Instagram at @thecolorcode.collective with your chapter and date. More about chapter color night.

Do you track how I use the site?

We record how visitors use the site and app, such as which pages you visit and actions like signing up or getting your analysis, to improve it. We never record what you type, your password, payment details, or the contents of any message.

Can I ask for a redo if my result looks off?

Yes, retake it any time, free. For the best read, take a new selfie facing a window, bare-faced or in light makeup; lighting and foundation are the most common reasons a photo reads differently. If it still looks off, message us on Instagram at @thecolorcode.collective and we'll take a look.

How do I know if I'm warm or cool?

Our quiz checks four signs: your veins, gold or silver, white or ivory, and how you react to the sun. Read how to tell if your undertone is warm or cool.

How do I get my review posted publicly?

Only with your consent, and only after a person on our team reviews it. See the Customer Reviews page.
